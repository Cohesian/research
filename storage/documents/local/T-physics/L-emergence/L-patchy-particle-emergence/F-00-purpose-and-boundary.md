# Emergence from Local Rules to Global Observation

## The question

This Lecture studies one precise version of emergence:

> Can particles that know only their own state and nearby interactions produce
> collective structures whose behavior is visible at a larger scale?

No particle receives a target graph. There is no instruction to form a chain,
a branch, or a cluster. A particle only translates, rotates, excludes overlap,
interacts through its oriented surface patches, and exchanges energy with a
thermal bath.

If a connected structure appears, its complete shape was not stored in any one
particle. It arose from the repeated composition of local dynamics.

## Three levels of description

Let the complete microscopic state be

$$
X(t)=\bigl(s_1(t),s_2(t),\ldots,s_N(t)\bigr),
$$

where $s_i(t)$ contains particle $i$'s position, velocity, orientation, angular
momentum, and fixed type. The same system can then be described at three
levels.

### Microscopic dynamics

At this level, the model contains particles, pair potentials, forces, torques,
noise, barriers, and a numerical time evolution. For a finite interaction
cutoff $r_c$, particle $i$ has neighborhood

$$
\mathcal N_i(t)=
\left\{j\ne i:\|\mathbf x_j(t)-\mathbf x_i(t)\|<r_c\right\}.
$$

Its conservative force is assembled from only that neighborhood and the local
walls:

$$
\mathbf F_i^{\mathrm C}(t)
=
\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i(t)}\mathbf F_{ij}(t)
+\mathbf F_i^{\mathrm{wall}}(t).
$$

The implementation may use a global neighbor-list data structure to discover
$\mathcal N_i$ efficiently. That is a computational index, not extra global
knowledge assigned to a particle.

### Mesoscopic structure

An external observer can project a saved microscopic state into a contact
graph:

$$
\Pi_G:X(t)\longmapsto G_t=(V,E_t).
$$

Particles become vertices. A pair becomes an edge when it is close enough and
its patches face one another. Connected components, chains, branches, and
cycles then become graph properties. This scale is intermediate: it forgets
exact velocities and coordinates while retaining collective structure.

### Macroscopic observables

A second projection reduces the graph and thermodynamic state to a small
observable vector:

$$
\Pi_M:X(t)\longmapsto
M(t)=
\bigl(S_{\max},\langle k\rangle,\Psi_2,U,K,T_{\mathrm{kin}}\bigr)_t.
$$

This vector records the largest connected fraction, mean degree,
orientational order, potential energy, kinetic energy, and kinetic
temperature. It is not a complete state. It is a question-specific view of
the system.

The full passage is therefore

$$
\boxed{
\text{local particle dynamics}
\longrightarrow
\text{contact topology}
\longrightarrow
\text{macroscopic measurements}
}.
$$

## The observer is not the controller

HOOMD-blue advances the complete simulation and Research analyzes its saved
trajectory. That does not make the particles globally informed. Observation
would become control only if an aggregate measurement were fed back into the
particle update rule. This experiment does not do that.

Likewise, a shared timestep does not violate spatial locality. All particles
advance on one numerical clock,

$$
X^{n+1}=\Phi_{\Delta t}(X^n),
$$

but each finite-range pair contribution still depends only on the relevant
local states. Synchronous time prevents the arbitrary order of a Python loop
from letting one particle see another particle's future during the same step.

## Why one animation is insufficient

One visually compelling trajectory establishes only that one event occurred
under one initial condition and one random sequence. It does not establish a
law. With seed $r$, write the observable as $M^{(r)}(t)$. An ensemble estimate
is

$$
\overline{M}_R(t)=\frac1R\sum_{r=1}^{R}M^{(r)}(t).
$$

The variation across seeds matters as much as the mean. A candidate effective
description becomes interesting when a projection is reproducible across
initial conditions, numerical resolutions, durations, and system sizes.

This first study uses only three seeds per condition. It is enough to expose
large seed sensitivity and compare controlled conditions, but not enough to
infer an asymptotic law.

## Operational meaning of emergence

Within this Lecture, an observed structure counts as emergent when:

1. the global structure is not passed to any particle as a target;
2. it is generated by repeated finite-range interactions;
3. it is measurable through a declared higher-scale projection; and
4. changing a local rule changes the distribution of that measurement.

This is intentionally modest. Emergence does not mean that mathematical
information appears from nowhere. The global behavior follows from the full
dynamics, but it may admit a simpler and useful description at another scale.

## Scope of this Lecture

The Lecture follows one classical HOOMD-blue patchy-particle model from its
microscopic equations through a controlled $2\times2$ seeded experiment. It
does not model atoms, quantum fields, electron orbitals, or a measured chemical
substance. It does not include dynamic patch creation or the scalar substrate
explored by the separate local simulator.

The immediate goal is narrower: make one emergence claim inspectable from
model definition to numerical evidence.
